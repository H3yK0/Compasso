Many relapses happen late at night, sleepy and low on energy. That's no coincidence. A tired body changes the way the brain chooses, and that tips things in favor of the quick reward.

## Sleep: a tired brain chooses worse

In a study at the University of California, **23 adults** had brain scans after a normal night and after a night without sleep. Without sleep, the regions at the front of the brain, involved in weighing choices, worked less; deeper regions, involved in reward, responded more. And the **desire to eat high-calorie foods went up**.

The study is about food, not pornography, but it shows a general mechanism: **without sleep, "I want it now" gets louder and the brake gets weaker**.

The American sleep medicine societies recommend that adults sleep **7 hours or more** a night, on a regular schedule.

## Movement: a brake that works right away

A review of **14 studies** on smokers found that a single exercise session, sometimes just a few minutes long, **reduced the urge to smoke** and withdrawal symptoms, with an effect that lasted up to about **50 minutes** afterwards. Again: it isn't a study about sex, but urges work in a similar way, and moving your body is a way out that is always at hand.

For general health, the World Health Organization recommends that adults get **150 to 300 minutes a week** of moderate activity (or 75 to 150 of vigorous), plus strength exercises.

## In practice

- **Phone outside the bedroom** and a set time to go to bed. Night is the highest-risk time for many people.
- **Urge hit? Move:** a 10-minute walk, 20 squats, going up and down the stairs. Then breathe for 2 minutes.
- **Regular exercise** improves mood and sleep, which in turn protect your decision.
- **Careful with alcohol:** it knocks out the brake and worsens sleep.

> You don't need infinite willpower. You need a rested body and a plan for the hard moments.
