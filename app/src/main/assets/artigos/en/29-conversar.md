For many people, it's easier to have sex than to talk about it. Desires, insecurities, what works and what bothers them stay unsaid. And if you're trying to quit pornography, there's even more held back. Research suggests that opening this door is worth it.

## What the meta-analysis found

In 2019, researchers at the University of Texas pooled **48 studies** on sexual communication between partners. On average, talking more and better about sex was **associated with better sexual function** in practically every dimension studied:

- more **desire** and more **arousal**;
- better **lubrication** and better **erection**;
- easier **orgasm**;
- **less pain**;
- and better **overall sexual function**, where the association was strongest.

Some associations were stronger for women, such as desire and orgasm, and the overall effect was larger among **married** people. Since most studies look at a single point in time, the direction isn't certain: talking may improve sex, and better sex may make talking easier. Probably both.

## Why talking helps

Without conversation, each person tries to guess what the other wants, and the anxiety of getting it wrong grows (see the article on performance anxiety). Talking turns assumptions into information, lowers the pressure and builds trust.

> Intimacy isn't only being naked in front of someone. It's being able to say what you feel without fear.

## How to start

1. **Pick the right moment:** outside the bedroom, unhurried, without alcohol, at a calm time.
2. **Start with the positive:** "I really like it when…" opens more doors than "you never…".
3. **Talk about yourself, not the other person:** "I feel anxious when…" is easier to hear than "you pressure me".
4. **Ask and really listen:** "what would you like to be different?". Listening without getting defensive is half the conversation.
5. **Agree on something small to try**, like an evening of sensate focus, with no goals (see the article on sensate focus).

## And what about pornography?

Telling your partner that you're trying to quit pornography is a personal decision. Many people feel relief when they do, because the secret weighs on them and feeds shame (see the article on shame and guilt). If you decide to talk:

- explain **what you're doing and why**, without having to give every detail;
- make it clear that it's **your own project**, not a problem the other person has to solve;
- be ready for emotions, including difficult ones; give it time;
- if the conversation gets stuck, a **couples therapist** can help you pick it up again.
