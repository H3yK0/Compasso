Every strong urge makes the same offer: something good **now** in exchange for a cost **later**. When "later" feels distant and abstract, the offer becomes irresistible. Research shows that this balance between now and later has a lot to do with habits that are hard to quit.

## Discounting the future

Behavioral economists call it **delayed reward discounting**: the tendency to prefer a smaller reward now over a larger one later. Everyone does it a little; the question is how much.

A 2011 meta-analysis brought together **46 studies**, with **64 comparisons**, between people with addictive behaviors (alcohol, tobacco, other drugs, gambling) and comparison groups. In the studies using equivalent methods, people with addictive behaviors **discounted the future more**, with a medium effect size. The difference was **larger in clinical samples**, made up of people who met criteria for a disorder.

These studies show association, not cause: the habit might increase discounting, discounting might make the habit easier, or both. But they point to a target: **bringing the future closer**.

## Meeting your future self

In 2011, psychologist Hal Hershfield and colleagues did something curious. They showed participants, in virtual reality, **age-progressed images of their own faces**, and they could interact with this "future self". In four studies, those who went through the experience showed **a greater tendency to accept later monetary rewards** over immediate ones, such as saving more for retirement.

The idea: many people treat their own future self almost like a stranger. When that self becomes concrete and close, taking care of it starts to make sense.

> The urge speaks to the you of right now. Your plan speaks for the you of tomorrow morning.

## How to bring the future closer

1. **Think "tomorrow morning", not "someday".** How do you want to wake up? What would that person thank you for?
2. **Write a letter from yourself a year from now** to yourself today. What does it say? What does it ask?
3. **Decide for your future self in advance.** Blockers, the phone out of the bedroom and the smart control's waiting period are commitments made calmly, to protect the decisions made in a hurry.
4. **Make the future reward visible.** Your sky for the month, the day streak and your level in the Progress tab are the future arriving little by little.
5. **When the urge hits, wait 10 minutes.** Discounting is strongest in the immediate moment; every minute of waiting brings "later" closer.
