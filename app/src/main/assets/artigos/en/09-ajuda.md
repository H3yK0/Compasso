Compasso is a tool for training and support. It doesn't replace a professional, and sometimes the bravest step is asking for help.

## Signs that it's worth looking for help

- You've tried to cut down **many times** and couldn't.
- Use takes up **hours**, gets in the way of sleep, work, studies or relationships.
- You keep going **even when you see the harm**.
- You use mainly to **escape** sadness, anxiety or loneliness.
- You feel **great distress**, shame that won't go away, or thoughts that there's no way out.

These points echo the criteria for compulsive sexual behaviour disorder from the World Health Organization (ICD-11), but you don't need a diagnosis to deserve care.

## Treatments with evidence

The most studied approaches are **cognitive behavioral therapies**, including **acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT)**. In a clinical trial with **28 men**, 12 sessions of ACT cut the time spent watching pornography by **93%** at the end of treatment, compared with **21%** in the group waiting for care. It's a small study, but the result is encouraging and matches what is seen in other compulsive behaviors.

When anxiety or depression are also present, treating them too usually helps a lot.

## Who to talk to

- **Psychologist:** ideally with experience in cognitive behavioral therapy or ACT.
- **Psychiatrist:** if there's anxiety, depression or other symptoms weighing on your daily life.
- **Urologist or family doctor:** for questions about erections, ejaculation and general health.
- **Sex therapist:** a health professional trained in sexuality.

## Where to find it

- **Your doctor or local health service** is usually the way in, and can refer you to psychology and psychiatry.
- **University training clinics** in psychology often offer free or low-cost care.
- **Emotional support:** if you're in crisis, call your local emergency number or a crisis line. In the United States, call or text **988**; in the United Kingdom and Ireland, Samaritans answer on **116 123**, free, 24 hours a day. For other countries, findahelpline.com lists free lines.

> Asking for help isn't a failure of your effort. It's part of it.
