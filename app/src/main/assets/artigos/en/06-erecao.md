"Porn causes erectile dysfunction in young men" is a line you hear a lot. The studies tell a more nuanced story, and it's worth knowing it without panic and without dismissing it.

## What they found

- **Croatia, Norway and Portugal:** two surveys of nearly 4,000 men didn't find pornography to be an important risk factor for problems with desire, erection or orgasm. There was a small association only among the Croatians, and it didn't replicate.
- **Follow-up over time:** a study with several samples found no consistent link between **how much** one watches and erections. What did show up linked to erection difficulties was the person **feeling that their own use is problematic**, and even then with no evidence of cause over time.
- **International survey of men aged 18 to 35:** among those sexually active, about **21%** had some degree of erectile dysfunction, mostly mild. It was more common among those with more problematic use and among those who found real sex **less arousing** than pornography (about 2.3 times higher odds). Since it's a snapshot of a single moment, it doesn't prove cause; the authors call for follow-up studies.

## How to read these results

Putting the pieces together, current science suggests that:

- **watching**, by itself, doesn't seem to ruin erections for most people;
- the risk seems to lie more in **compulsive use**, in **depending on novelty and a screen** to get aroused, and in **performance anxiety**;
- erection difficulties have many causes: anxiety, depression, sleep, alcohol, medications, circulation problems, hormones.

> If arousal only shows up with a screen, that's a signal to train presence, not a verdict.

## What helps

- **Cut back on pornography** and the search for constant novelty.
- **Train arousal without a screen**, slowly and paying attention to your body. That's what Compasso's stop-and-start training does.
- **Take the weight off performance:** an erection isn't a test; anxiety is one of its biggest enemies.
- **See a doctor** (urologist) if the difficulty is frequent or started suddenly. Erections also depend on the health of your blood vessels, hormones and mood, and it's worth checking.
