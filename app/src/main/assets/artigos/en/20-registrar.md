Setting a goal is the beginning. Between the goal and the result there's a step many people skip: **looking often at where you are**. A large analysis of experiments showed that this step, on its own, makes a difference.

## What the meta-analysis found

In 2016, researchers from the universities of Sheffield, Leeds and North Carolina pooled **138 studies**, with **19,951 participants**, in which people were randomly assigned to receive, or not, a prompt to monitor their own progress: writing down what they ate, logging exercise, measuring something, counting days.

The results:

- The interventions **greatly increased** how often people monitored their progress.
- Monitoring more **helped people reach their goals**, with a moderate effect size (d = 0.40).
- The effect was **larger** when the information was **physically recorded** (written down, marked) and when it was **shared** or made public.

In other words: just "having an idea" of how you're doing isn't enough. Writing it down and, if it makes sense, telling someone makes monitoring stronger.

## Why it works

Tracking progress closes a loop: you see the gap between where you are and where you want to be, and that guides the next step. Without a record, memory plays tricks: good and bad days blur together, and a single slip can seem bigger than the whole month.

> What's measured with kindness becomes information. What's measured with cruelty becomes a reason to stop measuring.

## How to track without it becoming a punishment

1. **Do your daily check-in** on Lodestar. It takes seconds and feeds your week in stars and your sky for the month.
2. **Record stumbles as data**, not verdicts: time, place, how you were feeling, what came before. That's how patterns show up.
3. **Review once a week.** Look at the Progress tab: where did the week go well? Which time of day is riskiest?
4. **Record the small wins too:** an urge you rode out, an "I'm close" used early, a night with the phone out of the bedroom.
5. **If it makes sense, share** your progress with a trusted person. No details needed; the week's numbers are enough.

Lodestar's history stays only on your phone. You decide what to show, and to whom.
