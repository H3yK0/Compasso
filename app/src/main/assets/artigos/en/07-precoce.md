Many people feel they "finish too fast". That isn't always premature ejaculation in the medical sense and, even when it is, there are evidence-based ways to improve.

## The medical definition

The International Society for Sexual Medicine (ISSM) uses three criteria together:

1. **Time:** ejaculation always or nearly always happens **before or within about 1 minute** of vaginal penetration, since the first sexual experiences (the *lifelong* form), or there has been a **marked, bothersome drop** in time, often to **about 3 minutes or less** (the *acquired* form);
2. **Control:** being unable to delay ejaculation on all or nearly all occasions;
3. **Consequences:** distress, frustration, bother or avoiding intimacy.

In other words, it isn't just the clock: what defines it is the time **together** with the lack of control and the distress.

## Where "stop and start" comes from

In 1956, urologist James Semans described the technique of **stopping stimulation** when the "point of no return" feeling gets close, waiting for it to subside, and starting again. Later Masters and Johnson popularized a variation with a squeeze. Lodestar is built on this idea: rhythm, pause, breathing and the **"I'm close"** button.

## What the studies say

- **Behavioral therapy:** a Cochrane review considered the evidence still **weak and inconsistent**, with small studies. But, in one of the studies, behavioral therapy increased intercourse time by about **7 minutes** compared with people waiting for treatment. Combining techniques with medical follow-up tends to help more.
- **Pelvic floor muscles:** in a study of **40 men** with the lifelong form, 12 weeks of exercises helped **33** of them gain control; the average time went from about **32 seconds to almost two and a half minutes**. It's a small study with no comparison group, but the exercise is simple and safe.

## How to train day to day

- **Notice it earlier:** the training teaches you to notice rising arousal early. Tap "I'm close" without fear; using it early is a sign of control.
- **Breathe during the pauses:** a long out-breath helps lower arousal.
- **Train regularly, not in a hurry:** smart control spaces out releases so training doesn't become a marathon.
- **No porn while training:** the idea is to learn to feel your own body, not to depend on a screen.

## When to see a doctor

If the problem causes distress, started suddenly or comes together with erection difficulties, talk to a urologist. There are medical treatments that can be combined with training; a health professional is the one to prescribe and follow them.
