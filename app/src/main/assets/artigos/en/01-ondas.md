When an urge shows up, it usually brings a false promise: *"this will only get worse until you give in"*. That's not how it works. An urge is like a wave: it rises, peaks and, when it isn't fed, loses strength.

You don't need to defeat the urge. You need to **not obey it while it passes**.

## Why the urge grows so much

A well-studied theory of desire, the *elaborated intrusion theory*, explains the process in two steps. First comes an intrusive thought, often pulled in by a trigger: the phone in bed, boredom, an image. Then the mind starts to **elaborate** that thought, imagining scenes, sensations and the relief that would follow. It's this elaboration that makes the urge intense.

That's where the good news is: if imagination is the fuel, keeping your imagination busy with something else takes strength away from the wave.

## What the studies show

Researchers asked students to log their cravings on a handheld device for a week. Those who played Tetris for **3 minutes** when a craving appeared saw, on average, a drop of **13.9 percentage points** in its strength. This held for food, coffee, alcohol, cigarettes and also for activities such as **sex**.

Another study taught smokers to "surf the urge": to notice the impulse in the body, without obeying it and without fighting it. Right after the training, the urge wasn't weaker, but over the **following 7 days** that group smoked fewer cigarettes than those who didn't learn the technique. The urge was still there; what changed was the response to it.

> An urge is a noisy visitor. It can sit on the couch, but it doesn't have to drive the car.

## A 10-minute plan

1. **Name it:** "this is an urge, not an order". Just naming it creates some distance.
2. **Breathe for 2 minutes** with the out-breath longer than the in-breath (in Compasso: Refuge → Breathe).
3. **Keep your imagination busy** with something visual for 3 minutes: one of the Refuge games, a puzzle, drawing.
4. **Change places:** get up, drink some water, leave the room, turn on the light.
5. **Write down the trigger** once the wave has passed: time, place, how you were feeling. This feeds your plan (see the article on triggers).

If the wave comes back, repeat. Every wave you ride out teaches your brain that you can feel an urge without obeying it.
