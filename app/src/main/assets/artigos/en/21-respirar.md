"Take a deep breath" sounds like grandma's advice. But breathing is one of the few automatic functions of the body that you can also control on purpose, and that's why it works as a doorway to calming the nervous system.

## What the review showed

In 2018, Italian researchers reviewed the studies on **slow breathing**, below **10 breaths per minute**, in healthy people. Of almost 2,500 papers found, **15** met the quality criteria.

The most consistent effects:

- **Increased heart rate variability**, a marker that the body is more in recovery mode than in alert mode.
- **Changes in the brain's electrical activity**, with more alpha waves, associated with a relaxed, attentive state.
- On the psychological side, **more comfort, relaxation and vigor**, and **less anxiety, anger and agitation**.

## The longer exhale

In 2023, a Stanford University study randomly assigned participants to do, for a month, **5 minutes a day** of one of three breathing exercises or of mindfulness meditation. The exercise with a **prolonged exhale** ("cyclic sighing": two inhales through the nose, followed by a long exhale through the mouth) brought **greater mood improvement** and a **greater reduction in breathing rate** than meditation.

That's why Compasso's Breathe uses **4 seconds to breathe in and 6 to let the air out**: about 6 breaths a minute, with the longer exhale.

> You can't order anxiety to leave. But you can let the air out slowly, and your body gets the message.

## How to practice

- **When the urge hits:** 2 minutes in Breathe, following the waves. Focusing on the rhythm also keeps your attention busy.
- **Cyclic sighing:** breathe in through your nose, take a second short inhale to fill the rest of your lungs, and let it all out slowly through your mouth. Repeat for a few minutes.
- **Every day, not just in a crisis:** practicing when all is well keeps the path ready for when it isn't.
- **During training pauses:** the session's guided breathing helps bring arousal down before the next rhythm.

If you feel dizzy or tingly, go back to your normal rhythm. The idea is calm, not effort.
