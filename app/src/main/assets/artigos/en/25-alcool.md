"I was doing well, then I had a few drinks and…" It's one of the most common relapse scripts, and not only with pornography. The most accepted explanation isn't that alcohol "releases your true self". It's something more mechanical, and that makes it easier to plan for.

## Alcohol myopia

In 1990, psychologists Claude Steele and Robert Josephs brought together studies on how alcohol affects behavior and proposed the idea of **alcohol myopia**. Alcohol impairs perception and thinking in a specific way: attention gets **stuck on what's most immediate and eye-catching**, and it becomes hard to process what's further away, such as consequences, plans and values.

From this come three effects the authors describe:

- **More extreme behavior**, because alcohol switches off the inner conflict between "I want to" and "I shouldn't". The "I shouldn't" depends precisely on the more distant information.
- **Inflated self-evaluations**: people feel more capable or more in control than they are.
- **Anxiety relief**, when there's some distraction nearby: narrowed attention can't reach the worrying thoughts.

Notice: alcohol doesn't decide on its own what you do. It increases the weight of whatever is **most salient** at the moment. If the most eye-catching thing is a good conversation, fine. If it's the phone in your hand at two in the morning, the urge wins without a contest.

## What everyday life shows

In a study that tracked the desires of 205 adults in real time over a week, **alcohol consumption** was among the situational factors that changed the strength of desire and the success in resisting it (see the article on willpower).

> After drinking, your plan still exists. It's just too far away for your attention to reach.

## How to plan around it

1. **Decide before drinking.** What will you do when you get home? Agree on it with yourself while sober.
2. **Put the plan in the most eye-catching place.** Phone charging outside the bedroom before you go out; a note on the door; blockers turned on.
3. **Drink less, or don't drink,** on nights when you'll be going home alone.
4. **Go home with someone or let someone know** you got back. Human contact is a strong, good stimulus.
5. **Remember sleep.** Alcohol disrupts sleep, and the tired next day is another risky moment.

If drinking is frequent or hard to control, it deserves attention of its own. A health professional can help (see the article on getting help).
