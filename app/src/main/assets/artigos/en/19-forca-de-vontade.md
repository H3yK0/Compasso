For years, one idea dominated self-help books: willpower would be like a battery. Every time you resist something, you use up a little, and by the end of the day it's empty. That's why slipping at night would be normal. The idea is intuitive. But when it was tested more rigorously, it wobbled.

## The test in 23 labs

The phenomenon had a name: **ego depletion**. People did a task that demanded self-control and then did worse on a second task. Dozens of small studies seemed to confirm the effect, but later analyses raised doubts about its real size.

In 2016, **23 laboratories** in several countries jointly repeated the same protocol, agreed on and registered in advance, with **2,141 participants**. The effect they found was practically zero (effect size d = 0.04, with a margin that included zero).

That doesn't prove tiredness doesn't exist. Sleeping badly, being hungry or stressed does weigh on you (see the articles on stress and sleep). But the idea of a fixed tank that drains with every "no" you say turned out much weaker than it seemed.

## What happens in everyday life

Another study followed **205 adults** for a week with a device that beeped at random times. At each beep, they reported whether they were feeling any desire, of what kind, whether it conflicted with other goals and whether they tried to resist. That added up to **7,827 reports**.

Desires turned out to be **frequent, variable in strength and, for the most part, unproblematic**. Those that conflicted with other goals usually triggered resistance, with mixed success. And the situation mattered a lot: **alcohol**, **the presence of other people** and being around someone who had already given in to the same desire changed what happened.

## Where the real lever is

If self-control isn't a battery, the strategy changes. Instead of preparing to resist more, it pays to **need to resist less**:

- **Cut down your encounters with temptation.** Blockers, phone out of the bedroom, no browsing "just to browse" at night. Every situation you avoid is a battle you don't have to win.
- **Automate the right response.** "If… then…" plans decided calmly work better than deciding in the moment (see the article on triggers).
- **Mind the social context.** Notice with whom and in which settings the urge gets stronger, and with whom it gets weaker.
- **Watch out for alcohol**, one of the factors that most affects control (see the article on alcohol).

> People who seem to have a lot of willpower have often just arranged their lives so they need less of it.
